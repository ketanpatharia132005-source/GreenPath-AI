Sustainable Development Goals, also called SDGs, are 17 global goals created by the United Nations to solve major global problems such as poverty, hunger, quality education, clean water, climate change, inequality, and responsible consumption.

SDG 4 is Quality Education. It focuses on inclusive and equitable education and lifelong learning opportunities. A project related to SDG 4 can help students learn skills, get career guidance, or access educational resources.

SDG 7 is Affordable and Clean Energy. It focuses on access to affordable, reliable, sustainable, and modern energy. A project related to SDG 7 can help people save electricity, understand solar energy, or track energy use.

SDG 8 is Decent Work and Economic Growth. It focuses on productive employment, entrepreneurship, innovation, and decent work for all. A project related to SDG 8 can help students identify skills, prepare for jobs, or understand green careers.

SDG 9 is Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure. It focuses on building resilient infrastructure, promoting inclusive industrialization, and supporting innovation. A project related to SDG 9 can use technology to solve sustainability problems.

SDG 12 is Responsible Consumption and Production. It focuses on reducing waste, recycling, sustainable production, and responsible use of resources. A project related to SDG 12 can help people understand waste sorting, e-waste disposal, and sustainable habits.

SDG 13 is Climate Action. It focuses on taking urgent action to combat climate change and its impacts. A project related to SDG 13 can spread climate awareness, explain carbon footprint, or suggest eco-friendly actions.

Green skills are the knowledge, abilities, values, and attitudes needed to live in and support a sustainable society. Examples of green skills include climate awareness, waste reduction, energy saving, sustainable design, green entrepreneurship, environmental data analysis, and AI for sustainability.

AI can help sustainability by analyzing environmental data, predicting climate risks, optimizing energy usage, reducing waste, supporting smart agriculture, and creating awareness tools.

RAG means Retrieval-Augmented Generation. In RAG, the system first retrieves useful information from documents and then uses that information to generate a better answer. RAG is more reliable than a normal chatbot because it can answer using a specific knowledge base.

Another project is EcoSort AI Guide. It helps users understand waste categories, recycling, and e-waste disposal. It supports SDG 12 and SDG 13.

Another project is Smart Energy Awareness AI. It gives simple electricity-saving suggestions and explains clean energy. It supports SDG 7.

GreenPath AI is an AI-powered sustainability assistant that helps users learn about climate change, Sustainable Development Goals, carbon footprint, renewable energy, waste management, water conservation, air pollution, and sustainable lifestyle practices. The main purpose of GreenPath AI is to spread awareness about environmental problems and suggest simple practical actions that students, individuals, and communities can follow in daily life. GreenPath AI uses a RAG pipeline, where RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. In this project, when a user asks a question, the system first searches the knowledge file for the most relevant information, then retrieves useful content, and finally the AI generates an answer based on that retrieved information. This helps the system give answers from the knowledge file instead of guessing randomly.

Sustainable Development Goals, also called SDGs, are 17 global goals created by the United Nations to make the world better by 2030. These goals focus on important issues such as poverty, education, health, gender equality, clean water, clean energy, climate action, sustainable cities, responsible consumption, and environmental protection. SDG 13 is Climate Action. SDG 13 focuses on taking urgent action to fight climate change and its impacts. It encourages people, governments, schools, industries, and communities to reduce carbon emissions, improve climate education, increase renewable energy use, and prepare for climate-related disasters such as floods, droughts, heatwaves, and storms.

Climate change means long-term changes in temperature, rainfall, weather patterns, and natural systems. It is mainly caused by greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane, and nitrous oxide. These gases trap heat in the Earth’s atmosphere and cause global warming. The major causes of climate change include burning fossil fuels like coal, petrol, and diesel, deforestation, industrial pollution, vehicle emissions, excessive electricity use, waste generation, and overuse of natural resources. The effects of climate change include rising temperatures, melting glaciers, sea level rise, floods, droughts, heatwaves, extreme weather events, loss of biodiversity, damage to agriculture, water shortage, health problems, and displacement of people.

A carbon footprint is the total amount of greenhouse gases produced by a person, activity, organization, product, or community. It is usually measured in carbon dioxide equivalent. Daily activities that increase carbon footprint include using petrol or diesel vehicles, wasting electricity, using air conditioners for long hours, using plastic products, wasting food, using too much paper, and buying products that require high energy for production and transport. A person can reduce carbon footprint by walking, cycling, using public transport, carpooling, saving electricity, switching off lights and fans when not needed, using renewable energy, planting trees, reducing plastic use, recycling waste, avoiding food waste, and using reusable products.

Green actions are small steps that people can take to protect the environment and reduce pollution. Examples of green actions include switching off lights when not needed, using reusable bottles and bags, avoiding single-use plastic, planting trees, saving water, using public transport, recycling waste, reducing food waste, using digital notes instead of paper, and spreading environmental awareness. Students can contribute to sustainability by carrying reusable water bottles, saving paper, using public transport or bicycles, participating in plantation drives, reducing electricity use in classrooms and hostels, avoiding plastic, and encouraging others to follow eco-friendly habits.

Renewable energy is energy that comes from natural sources that are continuously replenished. Examples of renewable energy include solar energy, wind energy, hydropower, geothermal energy, and biomass energy. Solar energy is generated from sunlight using solar panels. Wind energy is generated using wind turbines. Hydropower is generated from flowing water. Renewable energy is important because it produces less pollution, reduces dependence on fossil fuels, lowers greenhouse gas emissions, and helps fight climate change. Using renewable energy is one of the best ways to create a cleaner and more sustainable future.

Waste management means collecting, separating, treating, recycling, and disposing of waste properly. The main types of waste include organic waste, plastic waste, paper waste, metal waste, glass waste, electronic waste, and hazardous waste. The 3Rs of waste management are Reduce, Reuse, and Recycle. Reduce means using fewer resources and creating less waste. Reuse means using items again instead of throwing them away. Recycle means converting waste materials into useful products. E-waste means electronic waste such as old phones, laptops, batteries, chargers, and electronic devices. E-waste should be handled carefully because it may contain harmful materials that can damage human health and the environment.

Water conservation means using water carefully and avoiding wastage. Water is a valuable natural resource, and saving water is important for people, animals, agriculture, and the environment. Ways to save water include closing taps while brushing, fixing leaking taps, using buckets instead of showers, collecting rainwater, reusing water for plants, avoiding unnecessary washing, and spreading awareness about water saving. Rainwater harvesting is the process of collecting and storing rainwater for future use. It helps increase groundwater levels, reduces water shortage, and supports sustainable water management.

Air pollution happens when harmful substances enter the air. These harmful substances include smoke, dust, harmful gases, vehicle emissions, industrial pollutants, and chemicals. The main causes of air pollution include vehicle smoke, factory emissions, burning waste, construction dust, fossil fuel burning, and excessive use of polluting machines. The effects of air pollution include breathing problems, asthma, lung diseases, poor visibility, acid rain, global warming, and damage to plants and animals. Air pollution can be reduced by using public transport, planting trees, avoiding waste burning, using clean energy, reducing vehicle use, maintaining vehicles properly, and controlling industrial pollution.

A sustainable lifestyle means living in a way that reduces harm to the environment and saves resources for future generations. A sustainable lifestyle includes using reusable products, saving electricity, saving water, reducing plastic use, eating local and seasonal food, avoiding food waste, recycling materials, using eco-friendly transport, planting trees, and making responsible choices. Sustainability is important because natural resources are limited, and people must use them carefully so that future generations can also live safely and comfortably.

GreenPath AI is useful because it gives simple, practical, and educational answers related to sustainability. It helps users understand environmental problems and motivates them to take small actions that can create a positive impact on the planet.
